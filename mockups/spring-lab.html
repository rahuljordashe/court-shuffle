Springs, four places — pick a strength for each.

The “Deal” motion system in index.css bans overshoot on purpose (“nothing overshoots”), and the brief lists a bouncy app as an anti-reference. So every spring here ships in three strengths: Damped (a spring with no visible bounce — safe everywhere), Soft (one gentle overshoot — tactile), and Lively (a pronounced overshoot — the edge of on-brand). Springs are pure CSS linear() easings generated at load — no library, no bundle cost, and they collapse correctly under reduced motion. Drive every demo directly (tap a tab, a stepper, the button, a chip), or use the row's “Fire row” to play all three in sync. Section 5 answers the “generate feels too quick” question — that one is a pacing fix, not a spring.

How to read this: Damped is a real spring tuned to critical damping — it never crosses its target, so it is courtside-safe on any control. Soft and Lively cross once and settle. Reduced motion (toggle above) collapses all of them to an instant state change. None of this touches the app's data-testid hooks — it is transform and easing only.
My recommendation — tell me if you disagree
  • 1 · Tab indicator → Soft. Motion is missing here entirely; a slide wants a little life. Damped reads as plain ease; Lively bounces the most-seen control in the app.
  • 2 · Stepper → Soft. Direct manipulation — a small spring-back under the thumb feels tactile and earned.
  • 3 · Hero button → Damped. Generate / End Round is the referee's-whistle action. It should feel decisive, not playful. A firm spring-back, no bounce.
  • 4 · Pool chips → Soft. A toggle tick with one overshoot reads as a confident click.
  • 5 · Round reveal → Relaxed. The real fix is not just slower cards — it is pulling the title and sit-outs into the stagger so the whole round assembles instead of two cards flickering.
